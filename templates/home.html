Think about a pink elephant. Do you see it in your mind's eye? We are all different in what we see in our mind's eye. Mental imagery (imagining images, sounds, smells, etc.) varies remarkably between people. Some people experience no imagery at all — aphantasia — while others have imagery as vivid as real perception — hyperphantasia. Most people fall somewhere in between.

The Phantasia Research Hub is the most comprehensive and wide-ranging research project examining these extremes of mental imagery. We aim to understand how visual imagination interacts with broader cognitive capacities — memory, creativity, and other mental processes — to shape our inner experience. Our research investigates several related questions:

  • Are there subtypes of aphantasia and hyperphantasia, and, if so, what are their characteristics?
  • How do imagery extremes influence other areas of thinking?
  • How do imagery extremes interact with mental health?

This Hub is a first step in answering those questions, and many more.

Whatever you experience when thinking about a pink elephant, you can contribute to the Phantasia Research Hub. We are the Edinburgh University 'Eye's Mind' research team, and we look forward to working with you as we advance our understanding of the human mind.

About//The Eye's Mind Study

The Eye's Mind research group explores the basis of mental imagery diversity and its role in people's lived experiences and cultures. Our project builds upon our previous ground-breaking work to examine imagery extremes in rich detail. We study links between our brain, our mind, our experiences, science, and art. We aim to address 3 main goals:

  1. 1

    Understand the cognitive profiles and experiences of people with aphantasia and hyperphantasia. We know that aphantasic and hyperphantasic people are not a monolith, and there are many differences within each group. We want to understand whether different kinds of aphantasia and hyperphantasia exist, and if so, how these different kinds are alike and different.

  2. 2

    Understand the relationships between mental imagery and other psychological domains (i.e. memory, spatial navigation, neurodiversity, mental health). We also wonder whether some of these relationships are unique to aphantasia and hyperphantasia.

  3. 3

    Identify which areas should be the priority for the next phase of the international research community focused on imagery extremes. Aphantasia and hyperphantasia have only been named in the last 10 years. They are both still drastically under-researched, but the field is gaining more interest every year. Everything is still left to be discovered, and this project will help us all know which topics should be researched the most urgently.
