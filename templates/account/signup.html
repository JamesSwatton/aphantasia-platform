Become a participant

Welcome to the Phantasia Research Hub! Before you decide whether to join the Phantasia Research Hub, you must understand why we are doing this research, and what it involves. Please make sure you take the time to read carefully the following information before deciding to join or not. If you have any questions, you will find our contact details below.

About us

We are the Eye's Mind research group, and we are based at the University of Edinburgh. You can find our team's website at medicine-vet-medicine.ed.ac.uk/eyes-mind. As we are launching the Phantasia Research Hub in 2026, the lead researchers on this project are Dr Bérengère Digard and Professor Adam Zeman, in collaboration with Dr Edward Silson. Dr Digard's research focuses on neurodiversity, Professor Zeman's on cognitive and behavioural neurology, and Dr Silson's on visual perception. You can find out more about each of us on our profiles on the University of Edinburgh website.

We developed this research project with the guidance of a steering group of 5 aphantasic and hyperphantasic volunteers. They suggested topics to research and features to include in the Hub, and they gave feedback on the research project as a whole and on more specific details as well. This research is funded by the Tibore Foundation.

About the research

Think of a rainbow. When you do, can you see the rainbow in your mind's eye? Mental imagery is the mental mechanism we use to imagine pictures, sounds, etc., in our mind. We are all different in what our mental imagery creates, and we range from people with little to no imagery (aphantasia), to people with imagery as crystal clear as real life (hyperphantasia).

The aim of the Phantasia Research Hub is to better understand these extremes of mental imagery. There is still very little research available on aphantasia and hyperphantasia, which were only named in 2015. Right now, it seems that people in these extremes also differ from typical imagers in other ways, such as the way they remember their own life, the way they dream, the way they navigate through space, or the way their brain makes sense of sensory information — but we know this thanks to very limited data. In addition, we still don't know why, or how that happens, or how these different brain mechanisms work together.

With the Phantasia Research Hub, we will start answering all these questions.

If you decide to take part, you will create your own personal account in the Phantasia Research Hub, and you will access a large selection of questionnaires and tasks (similar to computer "games") that have been created specifically to measure each of these different features about your mind. There are measures of mental imagery of course, as well as measures of creativity, emotion, face recognition, future thinking, general intellectual skills, memory, mental flexibility, mental health, mind wandering, neurodiversity, personality, personal values, reading habits, sensory processing, sleep, social understanding, spatial navigation, and spirituality.

Everything is online and self-paced, which means that you will be able to log in and complete the measures whenever is best for you. These measures will each take 1 to 10 minutes to complete (4 minutes on average). You are free to complete or ignore any of the measures.

Criteria for taking part

  • You must be 18 years old or over.
  • You must be able to understand English well as all the measures are in English; you must not use automatic translations as they are not acceptable in research.
  • You must have good internet access on a computer (laptop or desktop) as the measures will not work well on a phone or tablet.

You can join regardless of what your mental imagery is like. If you have very faint imagery, you can take part. If you have life-like imagery, you can take part. If you are not sure what kind of imagery you have, you can take part. If you think you have very "normal" imagery, you can take part too.

Benefits and risks

This study will give you the opportunity to contribute to a completely new research programme at the very forefront of aphantasia and hyperphantasia research. There are no financial benefits to taking part, but you will get to see your scores in some of the measures, and you will get to know the in-and-outs of this research before anybody else.

In terms of risks, we expect that some volunteers will find it uncomfortable to sit and stare at a screen for a long time. This is why we built the Hub so that you can stop and log back in whenever feels most comfortable. More importantly, some of the topics covered in the Hub can be quite uncomfortable and distressing to think about, especially the questionnaires about mental health. Remember you do not have to complete any measure that you do not feel ready to do.

Protection and use of your data

We will be managing your data following the Data Protection Law. We will not share your name or contact details with anyone outside of the research team. When the data is exported outside the Hub for us to analyse it, your name and contact details will not be exported, only your anonymous participant number. The Hub's data will be stored on an encrypted server from the University of Edinburgh.

This study was approved by the University of Edinburgh's PPLS Ethics Committee. To contact the Committee, you can email ppls.ethics@ed.ac.uk. To access your data or if you are not happy with the way we use your data, you can email dpo@ed.ac.uk.

Voluntary participation and the right to withdraw

Your participation in this study is voluntary: you are free to join or not, you are free to complete each measure or not, you are free to stop a measure before finishing it. You are free to change your mind and to leave the Phantasia Research Hub at any point by deleting your account. If you decide to delete your account, all your data will be deleted from the database. However, if some of your anonymised data has already been exported for analysis, it will still be included in that analysis.

Questions

For any questions you have about the research project or the Phantasia Research Hub, please do not hesitate to email Dr Bérengère Digard at berengere.digard@ed.ac.uk.

Thank you for your help with this research — we would not be able to do any of it without your support.
